# Chapter Six — The Returned

*(Book version, tight third on Cassian. Written to VOICE-AND-CRAFT.md and the Book One throughline. Reach 1, Oppidum arc, chapter 6 of 7. The Reckoning, and the payoff of the Prologue: the man the Hound turned comes home wearing his own face, because his face is the weapon. Cassian does not know him. The reader does. The town's last hope curdles. Heavy by design; warmth comes only as the dog and the candle.)*

The town went up onto the wall to watch him come, and Cassian went with them, and he did not understand what he was looking at until he understood it all at once.

It was a man walking up the lake shore in the grey dawn, and there was nothing strange about the man except everything. He came on at the pace of a person with nowhere he needed to be and forever to get there, one sure step and the next and the next, and he did not slow on the rough ground and he did not hurry on the smooth, and Cassian had seen exactly one other creature move like that, in a mirror, in the moment the Milestone poured the road into his legs. The walk of a vessel. The walk that does not tire. Cassian's own walk, worn on a stranger like a borrowed coat.

Beside him on the wall an old woman had started to cry, the boneless helpless crying of someone whose body has decided the matter before the mind can argue, and she was saying a name into her hands, over and over, and the name went down the wall from mouth to mouth and changed every face it touched. Brand. Brand. It's Brand. And down in the chapel, Cassian would learn, a candle had been kept lit on a little shelf for years, the town's own small defiance of its own grief, for the lad of Oppidum who had walked out the gate to free the dead and never come back, the local boy they had half made a saint of, the hope they had buried without a body and lit a flame for anyway.

Cassian did not know the name. He had never heard it. But he watched it move through the people on the wall like cold water, watched it turn the living grey, and he understood that whatever was walking toward them had been theirs, and loved, and mourned, and that this was going to be worse than the Hollow and worse than the dreaming, because you can fight a thing in the dark and you cannot fight a thing that the whole town is weeping with joy to see.

Then Brand came close enough to see his eyes, and the weeping stopped.

Cassian had braced for a monster. A monster would have been a mercy. What came up the shore was a man, a real one, a big plain road-worn man not so unlike Cassian himself, and that was the horror of it, because the face was Brand's face, the face on the candle-shelf, the face the old woman had loved, and behind the face there was nothing at all. The eyes did not blink. They were not cruel. Cruelty would have been something. They were hollow, scraped out, two windows onto a house with no one home, and they found the crowd on the wall and the crowd on the wall flinched back as one animal, because every soul up there knew, without a word, that the thing wearing Brand had been emptied of Brand a long time ago, and a long way off, and had been sent the whole way home to them on purpose.

Captain knew first. The three-legged dog who loved every drunk and thief and stranger in Oppidum, who had eaten Cassian's stew off his own plate the first night, came up onto the wall, took one look at the thing on the shore, and began to make a sound Cassian had not known a dog could make, a low broken keening, backing away with its hackles up and its tail tucked, and then it turned and fled into the town and did not stop. After that, no one needed telling.

"Hello, Edra," said the thing that had been Brand.

A woman near the gate made a sound like she had been struck. She was perhaps thirty, hard-handed, handsome, the kind of face that had decided a long time ago to stop hoping for things, and Cassian watched the decision come undone on it and then watched something worse happen, watched her realise, in the space between one breath and the next, that the thing on the shore had said her name in Brand's voice and with Brand's mouth and with no more feeling than a door has when it remembers to open.

"You told me to come back," Brand said, to her, to all of them, in a voice with nothing standing behind it. "Famous or not at all, you said. So. I walked all the way to the end of the Road, Edra, to the place you lit your little candle toward, where they keep the dead." He did not smile, quite. His face only arranged itself near a smile and stopped there. "You want to know what's at the end of it. What I found, that all of you wept for. Nothing worth the walk. No one waiting to be freed. No one coming. The cold won this an age ago, and we are only the part of the world that has not had the sense to admit it yet." He spread his empty hands, almost gently. "I didn't come to frighten anyone. I came to do you a kindness, the last one there is. The hope is the thing that hurts you. I know, I carried it two hundred miles. Set it down and the hurting stops. That is everything I learned. I walked to the end of the world to learn it, so you would not have to."

And the worst of it, the part that made it the Hound's true work, was that the town wanted to believe him. Cassian felt it go through the crowd like warmth through cold limbs, the awful relief of it, because this was not a stranger's lie from the chapel steps, this was Brand, their Brand, their own brave hope come back from the very end of the Road to tell them, kindly, that they could put it all down now. Half the wall swayed toward him. The old woman stopped weeping and got a soft far look, that look, the dreaming look, and so did the man beside her, and Edra at the gate took one step down toward the shore with her hand out.

He got down off the wall. He did not draw the sword. Four nights with Maren's cold hands in his had taught him the sword was no use against this, and so he put himself between Brand and the gate and reached for the man the way he had reached for her, with noise and stubbornness and the flat refusal to let go, and said, "You're Brand. You're Brand of Oppidum and these people lit a candle for you for years, and whatever's wearing you now didn't walk that Road, you did, and I'm not going to let you stand here and undo the one brave thing this town has left."

For just a moment, something moved behind the hollow eyes. Not Brand. Cassian would tell himself later it was Brand, the last ember of him, but it was not, it was only the echo of a shape Brand used to be, and it looked at Cassian with something that might have been envy and might have been pity and said, in a voice gone suddenly and horribly its own, "You can't save the ones who already stopped, vessel. You can only stop the ones still walking. I stopped a long time ago. There's nothing in here to save." And then the hollow came back down over it like water closing over a thrown stone, and the thing reached out with a vessel's borrowed strength to take Edra by the wrist and walk her down into the cold lake, gently, kindly, the way you would lead a child to bed.

What happened then was fast and ugly and Cassian is not proud of the parts of it he remembers. He would not let it have her. The Hound had sent Brand home to do exactly this, to break the town's heart and drown its hope in front of it, and Cassian got between, and the thing that had been Brand fought him with his own kind of strength, tireless, untiring, two vessels in the shallows of a grey lake at dawn while a town screamed on the wall, and it did not want to win, Cassian understood, swinging, gasping, it wanted to lose, it wanted him to do the thing it could not do for itself. So he did it. There was no killing a man already emptied; there was only the mercy, the release, the last kindness left for a thing the dark had already finished, and Cassian gave it, in the cold water, with his grandfather's name in his blood and tears freezing on his face, and the town watched its hope die a second time, by the hand of the new hero they had not decided whether to trust.

Edra knelt in the shallows after and held the body and would not let anyone take it, and this time the candle would go out, and there would be a real grave, which was, Cassian supposed, the only thing he had given them. A real grave instead of a lit candle. He did not feel like it was enough. It was not enough.

He stood up out of the lake, soaked and shaking, and looked down the long grey shore the way Brand had come, and far off, at the place where the shore became the Road, a big grey man was standing, watching, who had not bothered to come closer because he had not needed to. He had sent a message and the message had been received. The Iron Hound lifted one hand, almost a salute, the unhurried courtesy of a craftsman acknowledging another's work, and then he turned and walked back down the Road, in no hurry at all, because he knew, the way he always knew, that the vessel would have to come to him now.

Oppidum was not Cassian's to save. It never had been. And staying would only fill its little chapel with more candles. He understood, standing in the freezing lake with a dead hero cooling in a weeping woman's arms, that the only way to stop the Road from coming to this town was to walk down it himself, and meet the thing at the end before it sent anyone else home wearing a face.

He went to find Maren, and the Vagabond, and the whisperer who sold maps. It was time to leave.
