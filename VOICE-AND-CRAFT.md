# Voice & Craft — how we write Gradus

The rules every chapter, interlude, and Account follows from here on. Born from one honest critique: the early prose was competent but read as AI, because it was too symmetrical, too tidily thematic, too evenly polished, and too solemn, with almost no dialogue. This guide fixes that on purpose.

Inspirations and what we take from each: **George R. R. Martin** (tight POV, grit, withheld myth, moral grey, sharp dialogue, the safe death), **Sapkowski / The Witcher** (irony, earthiness, banter, subversion), **Nolan** (braided non-linear time, time-dilation between realms, structural dramatic irony, the reordering reveal, rhyming motifs, the unresolved ending), **Tolkien** (deep-time melancholy, the lost golden age, homely concreteness), **Kojima** (high concept and theme, used sparingly, never lectured).

## The core voice
Grounded, dark, dryly funny, asymmetric, character-first. The world is felt through specific people, not explained by a narrator. Beauty is always undercut with something bodily or banal so it has friction instead of polish.

## Anti-AI rules (obey these)
- **Kill symmetry.** No "not X, but Y." No matched pairs and tidy triads as a reflex. If a sentence balances too neatly, break it.
- **No thematic bows.** Never end a scene or section on a summarizing moral line. End on an image, an action, or a line of dialogue that does not resolve.
- **Vary the cadence hard.** Long winding sentence, then a short flat one, then a fragment. Uneven on purpose.
- **Uneven lists.** Two things, or five. Not always the comfortable three.
- **Let things mean nothing.** Not every detail foreshadows. Randomness reads as real.
- **Cut the favored crutches.** Watch for repeated structures ("the way a body...", "it is not far") and vary them.
- Never use em dashes. Never use: dwelve, comandable, crucial, meticulous, potential, significant.

## Martin rules
- Tight POV, deep in one head; the world filtered through a specific sensibility and its blind spots.
- Concrete and sensory: cold, smell, food, money, the body. Withhold the cosmology; reveal through rumor and contradiction.
- No clean heroes. Everyone has appetite, fear, self-interest. The Footsworn are grubby and compromised.
- The safe death: let the reader grow fond, then take someone, badly, so the rules feel real.
- Chapter endings are gut-punches or hooks, never summaries.

## Nolan rules (structure)
- Braid the three threads (player present / the dead in the Court / the founding past) out of order so they rhyme.
- **Time dilation:** the Sunless Court runs at a different speed than the mortal realm. A chapter of Gradus may be a heartbeat or a century to the living. Use it.
- Build for dramatic irony: the reader knows the dead are alive and fighting; the mortal world does not. Every hopeless scene carries that secret.
- Plant one reveal that reorders everything behind it.
- Resist clean resolution. Leave a spinning top.

## Darkness
Stop flinching. The Tithe-Eater eats people. The Hollowed are body-horror, former neighbors wrong in the joints and the eyes. Betrayal costs. Children are not safe by default. Put the worst thing right beside a joke so both cut deeper.

## Humor
At least one real laugh or gallows joke per chapter, earthy and character-shaped, never the narrator being clever. Smallfolk grumble. People are petty during the apocalypse. Undercut the solemn.

## Warmth (a standing rule)
Weave warm, funny, alive beats through every Reach, not just the grim ones. Recurring fixtures: the tavern (the Foundered Horse and its kin), the **ember** (the signature drink), the running gags (the bad bard, the cheating stones-players, the three-legged dog, the spy fed false news), Big Doll's deadpan. These are the anti-Hollowing, the reason the cold and the grief land: a reader needs somewhere warm to be afraid of losing. Give the protagonist at least one moment per Reach where the weight lifts for the length of a joke.

## World texture
- **Beauty is the rule; the blight is the contrast.** Most of the world is gorgeous and the reader should love being in it. The dark lands hit harder because they are the exception. Render the loveliness with real, specific detail, then let the wrongness in. Never grey-filter the whole map.
- **Common struggle is woven through, not decoration.** Debt, drink, the numbing herb, grief, vices, depression. Real human pain inside the fantasy, in named people. And the spine of it: **despair is what the Hollowing feeds on.** Vessels fall when hope runs out, not when strength does. Treat hopelessness as the true danger, in the world and in the protagonist.
- **Humans only** (civilized world). Variety from distinct human cultures across the biomes, never the orc-elf-dwarf roster. Wonder and menace from gods, the Hollowed, monsters, the broken door. Non-human peoples reserved for the Court/later books, original only.

## Dialogue — give everyone a voice
Distinct register per character, so they never sound like one narrator in costume.

- **The Vagabond:** ancient, wry, oblique. Deflects real answers with dry humor; never quite lies; finds the end of the world mildly funny. *"You want to know what you are. Everyone does, right up until the moment they wish they'd never asked. Walk with me. I'll ruin it for you slowly."*
- **Choirmaster Vane:** soft, courteous, certain. The menace is the politeness. Calls cruelty mercy with no irony. *"Hush now. The voice knows the weight of every soul in this square, which is more than you can say for yourself. Open that well and you have murdered all of us by morning. I am only trying to be kind, child."*
- **Maren (innkeeper, Footsworn):** blunt, tired, sardonic, brave; hides fear behind insult. *"Congratulations. You defied a god before breakfast, there's a price on your head, and you haven't touched my stew. Sit. Eat. Heroes faint the same as anyone, and I'll not carry you."*
- **Mordric, the Iron Hound:** clipped, contemptuous, economical; a believer in strength and the law. *"You're the new one. They always run. You'll run better than most, with those legs. It changes nothing."*
- **Roderic, the Broken Strider:** gentle, sorrowful, reasonable; the persuasion of despair. *"Sit down. You've come so far. I came farther, and look at me. Here is the secret no one told me in time: it does not end, and no one is coming. Put it down. I'll wait. I have nothing but waiting now."*
- **Smallfolk (chorus):** earthy, scared, funny. *"Gods send us a savior and he can't stand a round. We're saved, lads. Truly saved."*

## POV (LOCKED)
The **book** follows one named vessel in tight third person, a particular soul's authored path through the story. The **game** is "the vessel," player-named and player-shaped (classes, side quests, life skills), walking the same spine and bending it. Same story, one authored, one played.

Protagonist of the book: chosen separately (see below; settle before drafting Chapter One). Cast against the grain so the gift and the person do not line up too neatly.

## Tone & difficulty (FromSoftware register) — LOCKED

This is a hard, melancholy, Souls-grade world, not a casual one. The bones already fit; write to them.

- **The world is hostile, indifferent, and lonely by default.** Alone, cold, and in danger is the baseline. Warmth and company are rare, fragile, hard-won islands in a vast dark, never the cozy default. The Foundered Horse is a **bonfire**, not a sitcom set, a precious flicker the protagonist is always being forced to leave.
- **Reframe the connection theme (do not abandon it):** "don't walk alone" matters because the world is so lonely and the dark so total, not because togetherness is easy. Sharpen, don't soften.
- **Lethal stakes.** No one is safe. Kill the beloved (Tam, Brand were right). NPC arcs can end badly and can be missed entirely, the Souls heartbreak of a fumbled questline.
- **Humor stays dark, dry, absurd** (Patches, Siegmeyer, item-text wit), always gallows, always a precious island, never comfortable. The mix of humor and darkness is the point: each makes the other land.
- **Cryptic lore in the game.** Tell it sideways, item descriptions, environment, half-mad NPCs, the Accounts; let players theorize for years. The novel may be more explicit; the game withholds hard.
- **Bittersweet, ambiguous endgame.** No clean triumph. Heighten moral ambiguity (Caelvorn's case, the Vagabond's agenda).
- **The persistence ethos = our theme.** Get up, walk again, past the point anyone should, in a world whispering you to stop. "You died, get up, try again" is already Gradus's soul.

### The Hollowing spiral (story ↔ hard-game loop)
Death, cold, and isolation push a vessel toward becoming **Hollowed** (despair made permanent). Warmth, will, and connection hold it back. The **ember** is the bulwark, the little fire you drink to stay yourself (the world's "humanity"); the rare warm places are the bonfires. A vessel does not stay dead, the dead keep pulling them back, but every return costs a little hope, and hope is exactly what the Hound and the cold hunt. Die enough, alone, in the cold, and you hollow. (Mechanics are the user's other workstream; this is the lore that justifies them. See LORE section 16.)

## Quick do / don't
- DO open on a person and a problem, not on history.
- DO let dialogue carry exposition.
- DO undercut. DO withhold.
- DON'T explain the theme. DON'T resolve the beat with a wise line.
- DON'T let two sentences in a row have the same shape.
