# Gradus — Characters (working draft)

Companion to LORE.md. Physical detail, bearing, and the one contradiction each principal carries. The aim is depth, not a hero-and-villain chart: every figure here should be arguable.

---

## Maelis

Appearance (LOCKED seed, expanded): pale, almost moonlit skin; eyes a clear cold green; hair the pale gold of winter wheat, worn in a single braid. Striking past anything the village of Wendholt could account for.

But her face is not the thing that stops you, and it is not what undid a god. Gods have beauty to spare; the nymphs of the upper realm are sculpted to it. What undid Caedmon was the way Maelis looked at him. Direct, unhurried, level, as if she had already seen the truth of him and was only deciding whether to be kind about it. A god has never been looked at that way by a mortal. He is used to being looked up at, worshipped, feared. To be seen, plainly and as an equal, by a farmwife with cold green eyes was a thing no god had ever survived, and Caedmon did not.

Craft: a healer and midwife of Wendholt. She read bodies the way she read faces, finding the hidden break, the quiet rot, the thing no one else had noticed yet. This is the root of the clarity that later becomes the Maelis vessels' gift: she sees the true state of a thing under whatever it shows the world.

Bearing: calm, plain-spoken, a little unbending. She did not perform for anyone, which is part of why Gradus loved her and part of why she stood so easily against a god.

Her flaw (rhymes with Gradus's): certainty. She trusted her own sight absolutely, and that made her proud in a quiet way and slow to ask for help. She crossed to the temple alone because she was sure she could face whatever waited there. Her sight was true. Her certainty that seeing the danger was the same as surviving it was not. The very gift that defines her is what walked her out onto the ice.

Open ambiguity (recommended, see LORE deepening B): never resolve whether some part of her was drawn to Caedmon. She refused him. Whether she felt nothing in the refusing is a thing even she might not have been able to say.

## Gradus

Appearance: tall and lean, built by work rather than war, with none of a warrior's bulk. Dark hair gone iron at the temples even before the Sentence. Weather-browned skin, a blunt plain face, calm grey eyes, hands and feet calloused to leather. The slow, unhurried way he moved made people take him for simple. After the Sentence the leanness hardened to corded iron, the grey held, he stopped aging, and now he moves like weather: never fast, never tiring, never stopping.

Bearing: quiet, literal, patient well past the point most people call stubborn. A man of few words who distrusts grand speech and gods in equal measure. Kind in undramatic ways. He showed Maelis love not with words but by walking the long way to bring her the clean water, by being exactly where he said he would be.

His flaw (LOCKED, section 7c): the conviction that endurance and force overcome anything. It is not arrogance. It is the only lesson his whole life ever taught him, and it has almost always been true, which is precisely why he cannot unlearn it, even though it killed a god, brought the Anathema down on the world, and now keeps him from finding a wife who can only be reached by standing still.

The question the player carries: hero, or the reckless force whose rage doomed everyone. The story never settles it.

## Families (LOCKED)

Timeline note: the founding tragedy lies hundreds of years in the past. The families all lived and died in that founding age and are long dust by the player's day. Their value is that they make Gradus and Maelis real people who came from somewhere, with the loved and the unloved that everyone has. Their lines may persist as scattered descendants (an optional thread), but no one alive knew them.

### Maelis's family (the healers of Wendholt)
- **Aregund**, her mother: the village healer, who taught Maelis the craft and the sight. Warm and formidable. (Loved.)
- **Osric**, her father: a gentle shepherd, died of a fever when Maelis was a girl. It is part of why she became a healer, to mend what took him. (Loved, lost early.)
- **Bertin**, her older brother: a woodsman, huge and fiercely protective, who could not bear that a god had looked at his sister and did something rash about it that cost him. (Loved, but his protectiveness was a cage.)
- **Wyn**, her younger sister: plain, raised in the shadow of the beautiful daughter, loved and resented Maelis in equal measure (the tangled real bond). She outlived Maelis and, before her own death long ago, left a testimony of her sister, grief and envy and love all tangled in it. That record survives as one of the Accounts (ACCOUNTS.md), the nearest thing to a true telling of Maelis, which is why the Wakeful prize it. So Wyn matters to the present not as a living NPC but as a centuries-old voice still half-trusted.

### Gradus's family (farmers of Wendholt)
- **Cuthwin**, his father: thought his son's endless walking shiftless and useless, never understood him, and died before the gap could close. (Strained.)
- **Hild**, his mother: warm and patient, a walker herself, who gave Gradus his patience and his love of the road. Died before the tragedy, spared the sight of what he became. (Loved.)
- **Aldred**, his brother: the sharp, ambitious one who stayed, worked the farm, and thought Gradus a fool his whole life. After the god-killing, when the name turned dangerous, Aldred was among the first to see that a martyr is useful, and helped turn the brother he scorned into a legend and a coin, out of opportunity, not love. So the cult of Gradus may have a self-serving man at its root, which poisons the question the whole resistance rests on: was the coin born of devotion, or one bitter brother's grift? (Unloved; the uncomfortable answer to "why the coin.")

### Caedmon's mortal pawns (origin of the quislings)
Caedmon did not sour the well with his own hands. He used men of Wendholt who served a god for favor, chief among them **Hewel**, who poisoned the well and spread the rumors for the god's promises. These first collaborators are the true ancestors, in spirit, of the quislings and the Hollow Choir. The shady characters were present from the very first crime.

## The gods

### Caelvorn the Sovereign, the High Crown

Appearance: he has no settled face, because he is the Voice. For a thousand years mortals knew him only as the Litany at dawn, a presence from everywhere and nowhere. When the gods take form among themselves he wears a king's: tall, grey-bearded, terrible and tired, a crown that looks fused to the bone.

Nature: order itself. Not cruel by temperament. For an age his voice fed and guided every mortal life, and the world prospered under it. He was a steward before he was a tyrant.

His contradiction (deepening A, recommended): his grief is real and his grievance is not empty. A mortal murdered his son. By the realm's oldest law no mortal may raise a hand to a god, and Gradus did, and now the world stamps the murderer's name on its coins and sings him in its taverns. From where Caelvorn sits he is not a tyrant tormenting innocents; he is a father and a king watching a death-cult grow around the man who killed his child, fighting to hold the order that kept mortals safe. He is wrong in what he does to the innocent. He is not wrong that he was wronged. The player should never be able to fully dismiss him.

### Seraphine the Kind

Appearance: warm and grave, a woman past youth with kind eyes and the stillness of a sickroom at night. She tends the dying, so death does not frighten her. Plainly dressed among the gilded gods.

Nature: mercy. The one god the dying have ever loved.

Her contradiction: she shelters the man who murdered her own son, because she knows her husband's wrath and her son's guilt and will not pretend otherwise. She grieves Caedmon, hides Maelis, and aids Gradus all at once, a heart pulled three ways, and every act of mercy costs her something she cannot get back.

### Valdric the Iron

Appearance: huge and scarred, a smith's burns up both forearms, a beard singed short, hands like the tools he made. He looks less like a god than like a veteran sergeant who has buried a lot of younger men.

Nature: earned strength, the law, honor. Hard and fair.

His contradiction: he upholds the law that condemns Gradus while secretly pouring his own strength into the man's road, because for the first time in his existence his creed and his heart disagree. Worse, his own son Mordric took that same creed of strength and turned it to domination in Caelvorn's service. The god of earned strength must watch strength corrupted in his own blood while the mortal who honored it best is the one the law wants dead.

### Myrren the Veiled

Appearance: veiled head to foot, the face never shown. Some say there is nothing beneath the veil; some say too many eyes. She speaks rarely and softly.

Nature: knowledge, the deep words, the source of magic. Curiosity without morality.

Her contradiction: she serves no side but her own hunger to know. A mortal who can kill a god is the most interesting thing to happen in an age, and she would let the world burn to see how it ends. The danger is that she might aid either side, whoever brings her nearer the answer, which makes her the one god no one can count as friend or foe.

### Aelric Far-Sight

Appearance: lean and restless, eyes pale and unfocused as a man staring at a horizon no one else can see. Never quite still.

Nature: foresight, the bow, distance. Lonely with knowing.

His contradiction: he saw all of this coming, warned the others, and was ignored, and it has quietly turned him against his own kind. He wants mortals free of the Litany, free of the gods entirely, and that cause is larger to him than any one town or any single vessel. His son walks the mortal world (the Vagabond) to serve it. Which means the player's gentlest-seeming ally answers to a god who may be willing to spend the player for the longer war. (See deepening C.)

## Cassian Strane (the book's protagonist) — LOCKED

The novel follows Cassian; the game is "the vessel," player-named (see VOICE-AND-CRAFT.md). Cast strong and noble, then complicated so he is not flat.

Who he is: a vessel of **Gradus** (the endurance gift). Strong in the truest sense, the kind of man others fall in behind without being asked. Upright, courageous, plain-spoken, and he genuinely believes the old ideals. Tight-third POV; narration leans earnest, which lets other characters' wit and the world's grubbiness do the cutting.

The inheritance (the twist): Cassian is descended from **Aldred**, Gradus's brother, who scorned the man alive and sainted him dead for profit. Aldred's line grew into minor gentry on the back of that grift, the **Strane** family, keepers and sellers of the Strider legend, who have named their heirs to honor it ever since. So Cassian carries a famous name his own blood cheapened, knows the legend is half a swindle, and has spent his life trying to become the real thing his ancestors only sold. Some folk (Footsworn especially) clock the Strane name and resent the family that "owns" the people's god.

His flaw: he must carry everything himself, must be the one who is worthy, and cannot let anyone else pay the cost. Not humility, a kind of pride, and it is the exact road that hollowed Roderic. People will die because Cassian would rather break than share his burden. The reader should admire him and want to shake him.

His wound (reveal slowly): somewhere behind him is a person he tried to save by carrying it alone, and lost. It is why he reaches for the name like a drowning man.

The charm (LOCKED, played light): women are drawn to him, the jaw of a temple saint, the eyes of a kicked hound, an earnest hero who listens, and he is genuinely innocent about it. He does not notice, does not pursue, assumes people are simply being kind. Played for warmth and comedy, never lechery; the obliviousness is the charm. A real love interest comes in a later Reach/book; for now it is the town fussing over him and Edra being magnificently unmoved.

The engine (withheld): the spirit of the real Gradus, the man Aldred betrayed, reaches across the centuries and chooses Aldred's descendant. No one knows why, Cassian least of all. Never resolve it into "because blood" (vessels are not a bloodline, LORE 7). The choice is pointed, strange, and the not-knowing is a hook.

## Edra (Cassian's first companion) — LOCKED

Introduced in Chapter Six, joins Cassian on the Road at the end of Chapter Seven, so he does not leave Oppidum alone (the first proof of his arc).

Who she is: a hard-handed, handsome woman of Oppidum, around thirty, who decided long ago to stop hoping and was proven horribly right. She loved Brand, the local boy who walked off to free the dead; she watched him come home Hollowed and watched Cassian give him the mercy-kill in the lake.

Why she comes: not for Cassian. Brand went down the Road to find what was at its end and came back wrong, and Cassian is the only soul she has met walking toward the same thing on purpose who might live to reach it. She needs to know what took Brand. And someone needs to walk behind the vessel and tell him "it isn't all right to stop" when the dark starts whispering. "That's the job. I'm taking it. You don't get a say."

Dynamic: dry, cutting, unsentimental, practical and tough (a fisher's hands, handy with a gutting-knife). The one woman immune to Cassian's charm, which she considers the only tolerable thing about him ("I would sooner kiss the Hound. We're going to get along beautifully"). She hates him "the right amount, which is not very much and will not go away." Grief-forged wit. Her own arc and stake. Whether it ever turns romantic is open and deliberately not now; for now she is the deadpan counterweight and the voice that keeps Cassian from his own despair.

## Hob, the hearthman (recurring cook) — LOCKED

A huge, scarred, gentle ex-soldier who lost his taste for war and now keeps a hearth on the Road, feeding the weary. Bridges two new pillars: food (he runs the cookhouse, tends an everpot, see LORE section 12) and armies (a man who has seen battle and walked away from it, whose stories foreshadow the war gathering down the Road). A recurring warm island, a bonfire that feeds you. Dry, unhurried, kind in a way that has clearly cost him. He fed both sides once, he says, and stopped being able to tell them apart, and that is why he only feeds people now. Carries a soldier's past he does not volunteer.

## A Footsworn war-leader (Reach 2 — to be named/developed)

The figure who turns the Footsworn from a frightened cell into a force in the Greenwood. A war-leader with a real plan and real costs (sends people to die for the cause). Foil to Cassian's reluctance to be a banner. Name and full character to be built when we write the Greenwood. Carries the armies thread (LORE section 17).

## The Choir's captain (Reach 2+ — to be named/developed)

The Hollow Choir's armed fist, a fallen paladin who leads its enforcers, the military threat distinct from Vane's soft pious menace and from the Hound. Antagonist for the army-scale conflict. To be built in the Greenwood. Possibly tied to Mordric.

## Orris the Inkfinger (the master of whispers) — LOCKED

The Varys figure: an information-broker whose loyalty no one can pin. Present in Oppidum from Chapter One.

Cover: a letter-writer. In a world where most folk cannot read and the dawn voice can no longer be trusted, the man who writes your letters and reads you the ones you receive knows every secret in three Reaches, and Orris has made that small trade into a web. He sits in the corner of the Inn with his portable desk, soft and plump and ageless, always pleasant, ink to the second knuckle (hence the name). He remembers everything and forgets nothing and never raises his voice, not even when what he is saying should make a man go cold.

Loyalty: genuinely unclear, and meant to stay that way. He trades secrets to the Footsworn, to the Choir, to travelers, to anyone, and claims, when pressed, to serve only "the living," the ordinary people who will still be here when the gods and their wars have burned out. Whether that is true or a beautiful cover, no one knows. He may be Wakeful, or feeding the Wakeful, or feeding on them. He is the one early figure the player cannot simply trust or distrust, and that ambiguity is the point.

Voice: soft, courteous, theatrical, deniable, faintly amused. *"Strane. Now there is a name I have written more than once, though never for anyone who looked as poor as you. Sit, sit. I'll not charge you for the chair. I charge for everything else, but never the chair."*

Chapter One: he clocks Cassian's surname at the Inn before the dawn bell, takes his measure, and files him away. He becomes a recurring broker the player must deal with across the Reaches, sometimes ally, sometimes leak, always for sale and never quite bought.

Signature item: his letters and ledgers (the web).

## Roderic, the Second Strider (the fallen legend)

Appearance: once, the very image of hope. A big, plain, road-worn man not unlike Gradus himself, which is part of why the songs called him the Second. Now, as the Broken Strider: the same frame gone grey and wrong, the vessel-gait turned mechanical and tireless, eyes hollowed to something that does not blink, the mark he once bore proudly now a scar that will not close.

Who he was: the greatest vessel before the player. A Gradus vessel who walked farther down the Road than anyone since Gradus, far enough that the Footsworn sang him as Gradus returned in the flesh, the one who would reach the Mile and free the dead. He carried a realm's hope.

His fall: he came to the Ruins seeking what the dead city knew, a way to the Court, a way to break the Anathema, and he tried to force it, the way Gradus would. Mordric the Iron Hound broke him there and turned him. He is the living end of Gradus's own flaw, force pushed past the point where it shatters.

His danger is not his strength but his argument. He is not mad. He preaches a coherent, sympathetic despair: that every vessel who fights only multiplies the world's suffering, that Gradus's defiance is what froze the world, that the merciful thing is to stop walking and let the gods have their order back. He offers the player that mercy before the fight, and the terrible part is that he is not lying about the suffering. He is only wrong about the answer. He is the warning the player carries down the rest of the Road: this is what I could become.

(Name adjustable. He is a Gradus vessel, for the direct mirror; a fallen Maelis-vessel counterpart could exist later.)

## Caedmon (the dead son)

Appearance: young and golden, beautiful like his mother, with the easy careless grace of a prince who has never once been told no.

Nature: a god-child who had never met death, consequence, or refusal. Not malicious. Careless in the particular way only the never-refused can be.

His contradiction (deepening B, recommended): did he love Maelis, or only want her? Leave it genuinely open. It may have been the first real feeling of his immortal life and he had no idea how to hold it without crushing it. It may have been pure appetite dressed as love. Even the gods do not agree. His tragedy is that the first true thing he may ever have felt killed the woman who caused it and got him killed in turn, and no one, mortal or divine, will ever be certain which it was.
