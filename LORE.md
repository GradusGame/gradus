# Gradus — Lore (working draft)

Status: in progress, updated 2026-06-13. We build this one step at a time. Names locked unless marked OPEN.

Inspirations in tone: Game of Thrones (power ruins from the edges), The Witcher 3 (grief, moral grey, careless cruelty), Elden Ring (a golden age already cracked), The Lord of the Rings (a lost order worth mourning).

---

## 1. The world as it stood

The realm: **Antevorta** (LOCKED). The name is borrowed from Roman myth, a goddess of the future, from *ante* (forward) + *vertere* (to turn): "she who turns forward / looks ahead." Fitting for a game about walking onward.

For longer than any chronicle records, mortals lived under the **Four Crowns**, one god per calling, all of them beneath one king:

- **Valdric the Iron** — hammered the first sword, watches over Knights.
- **Myrren the Veiled** — keeps the deep words, watches over Mages.
- **Aelric Far-Sight** — strung the first bow, watches over Archers.
- **Seraphine the Kind** — watches over Clerics and the dying.

Above them: **Caelvorn the Sovereign, the High Crown** (LOCKED), king of the gods and consort of Seraphine. His was the voice of the **Litany**. DECISION taken: Caelvorn is a fifth, higher god above the four, not one of the Crowns — because "the voice of the Litany itself turned against you" is a far larger threat than an angry peer.

The gods never set foot on the soil. They spoke only through the **Litany**, instructions read at dawn from temple steps, and that voice was Caelvorn's. The people obeyed without question. There was peace because there was no choice.

> Design note: open the game on this stillness. One quiet, almost dull dawn before everything breaks.

## 2. The three realms (cosmology — LOCKED)

The world has three realms. The fitness loop and the future expansion both live in this structure.

1. **The Realm of Toil** — the realm of effort, from which walking becomes power. (Design decision, LOCKED: the game is a complete self-contained RPG, and the player's in-game walking/effort is the default source of power. Real-world step-sync is an OPTIONAL bonus layer, the "pilgrim's path" buff, never a gate. The fiction covers both: the Milestone drinks "miles walked," in-game or real. The book has no steps. Walking stays the theme and soul regardless.)
2. **Viatera, the Trodden Lands** (LOCKED) — the game as played now. Proper name *Viatera* (from *viator*, traveler); common folk call it the Trodden Lands. Mortals take up the road here as **vessels** (see section 7).
3. **The Sunless Court** — heaven and hell combined (LOCKED). The realm of the dead AND of the gods, the single country every mortal goes to when they die, divided into regions under the different Crowns: some blissful, some torment, sorted by which god holds you, with Caelvorn the High Crown over all of it. Reserved for Book Two and beyond. Gradus is there now, and so is Maelis, but he cannot find her (see section 7b). A vast dark where the husband walks on, searching for the wife who is closer than he knows. (Because it is the afterlife of everyone, the war in the Court is a war over everyone's dead, which is the deep stake of the whole series.)

Maelis is there because she **died** (drowned in the ford) and all the dead go to the Court; Seraphine, who holds the gentle dead, caught and hid her (section 7b).

How Gradus reached the Court (LOCKED as a mystery, major future reveal): he did **not** die. Sentenced to walk without end, he walked past where the road should end and crossed into the Court alive, the only living thing ever to enter the country of the dead. That is what makes him unique, and it is a better answer than any bloodline. By what means he did it, not even the Four Crowns can say; they have argued it for an age. The true answer is withheld as a late reveal.

## 2b. The Elder Road (LOCKED — a power older than the gods)

The Milestone in Oppidum's Keep is older than the Litany, and likely older than the gods themselves. Before there were Crowns to bless anything, walking was already holy: the **Elder Road** ran, and the act of walking it answered with power. The gods did not make this. They inherited it, and built the Litany on top of it the way a temple is built on an older grave.

This is the deeper reason toil becomes power. Valdric's nightly gift (section 7) is real, but it rides on an older current that was always in the Road. The Milestone is a relic of that elder thing, which is why it hums for a vessel and why it predates every god who ever spoke.

It also reframes the ascension mystery (section 2): perhaps no god let Gradus cross into the Sunless Court. Perhaps he simply walked the Elder Road to its end, past where the gods' world stops, by a power that was never theirs to grant or deny. The true nature of the Elder Road (a place, a law, or a sleeping power, and whether it has a will) is withheld as a long-game reveal larger than the family feud.

## 3. The son who walked

**Caedmon**, son of Seraphine the Kind and Caelvorn the High Crown. (Irony: the child of the god of mercy commits the merciless act. Caelvorn and Caedmon share the "Cae-" root on purpose, father and son.) No god had ever walked the realm. Caedmon, bored of the perfect upper realm and its tame nymphs, slipped down to see what mortals made of their days.

> The medieval routine: walk him through one ordinary day in the village of **Wendholt** — bread baking, a miller arguing over the weight of a sack, children chasing a dog through mud. Caedmon finds the imperfection strange and beautiful. Then he sees **her**, and the day stops.

## 4. Maelis, and the man who walked

The woman: **Maelis**. The sculpted nymphs of the upper realm bored Caedmon; Maelis was real, and that undid him. She was already wed, to **Gradus**.

Gradus was no lord and no warrior. He was a man who walked — to the far field at first light, to the well, the long way to the hunting grounds when the short way would serve. He owned a good horse and rarely rode it. Folk called him slow and simple, a man who would wear out his boots before sparing them. Maelis loved him for exactly that. Caedmon was a god who never earned a single step; Gradus had earned every one.

> Theme engine of the whole game: a god who never walks versus a man who does nothing else. Steps as devotion, patience, the opposite of divine entitlement.

## 5. The undoing

Caedmon did not strike Gradus directly. Gods do not brawl with farmers; he ruined a life from the edges (the GoT register). He soured the well, emptied the hunt, whispered through the Litany that Gradus was cursed until neighbors crossed the road to avoid him. And he did not do it with his own hands: he used men of Wendholt who served a god for favor, chief among them **Hewel**, who poisoned the well and spread the rumors. These first collaborators are the ancestors in spirit of the quislings and the Hollow Choir (CHARACTERS.md). He meant to make Maelis a widow of circumstance, then arrive as comfort. It went wrong, and Maelis died.

> Timeline (LOCKED): the founding tragedy lies hundreds of years in the past. Gradus rose to the Court centuries ago and the Anathema has gripped the world ever since. Every soul who knew Gradus and Maelis in life, their families among them, is long dead. The tragedy is now deep legend, which is part of why the Accounts disagree so wildly: centuries of retelling have worn the truth past recovering, and many vessels have risen and fallen across the long span (Roderic among them).

How Maelis dies (LOCKED — merged frozen ford + the mercy that wasn't): Maelis learns a god has fixed his eye on her and is quietly unmaking her husband. She crosses the frozen river alone to reach the temple on the far bank, the one authority that sits above a single god's whim, to beg the Litany for protection. Caedmon has called an early winter that strands Gradus on the wrong side, so she goes without him. The god who answers her plea at the altar is Caedmon himself, wearing a borrowed face. She sees through him and refuses him to his face. Fleeing him back across the ice, the ford he froze gives way and she goes under. He never laid a hand on her; she died refusing a god. Gradus, racing the winter home, finds her beneath the ice.

> This version keeps the image (Maelis under the ice he made) and the temple betrayal, and it gives her agency: she acts and refuses, rather than waiting. See the review (section 10) on why that matters.

> Witcher register: Caedmon is a careless villain, not a cackling one. He never wanted her dead. He simply did not think a mortal life weighed anything.

> Ambiguity (LOCKED, deepening B): leave it genuinely unresolved in canon whether Maelis felt any pull toward Caedmon, and whether Caedmon loved her or merely wanted her. She refused him; whether she felt nothing in the refusing, no one can say. His feeling may have been the first true thing of his immortal life or pure appetite dressed as love. Even the gods do not agree, and the story never settles it.

## 6. The killing of a god

Grief turned to rage. No mortal had ever killed a god, and none was meant to be able to. But Caedmon broke the covenant first by walking the realm in flesh, and flesh can die. (Full dramatized version: Founding-The-Killing-of-the-God.md.)

The killing (LOCKED): the man who loved to walk and rarely rode his horse saddled it now and rode it full speed, against his own nature, because for the first time walking was too slow. That betrayal of who he was is the measure of his grief. He rode the horse to its death, then took the last ground on foot, refusing to stop, and the god who thought a horseless man would fall back found him still coming. The kill was graceless and human: a plain man with his grandfather's rusted sword, no warrior, hacking down a god by rage and refusal, not skill. The endurance theme survives in the final walk up the slope; the horse and the sword are the new spine.

> Contrast beat: the walker rides, and rides his beloved horse to death. The blade that does the deed becomes **Gradus's Sword** (see Signature relics, section 15).

## 7. The Sentence, the secret gift, and the vessels

The Four Crowns convened. A son of the gods was dead. They could have unmade Gradus. Instead **Seraphine the Kind**, grieving her own child yet seeing who broke faith first, argued for a sentence rather than erasure.

The judgment: **Gradus would walk the realm without end, a hundred thousand miles and more, never resting, until the gods relented.** They called it punishment. They meant it as a slow undoing. It did not undo him. Every mile made him stronger; his legs turned to iron, his wind grew bottomless. He carried weight, and the weight only fed him. The curse meant to break him built him instead.

### The two secret helpers (LOCKED, reveal late, plant clues early)

- **Valdric the Iron** folds his own strength into the road dust each night, so the curse builds the body. This is one reason real-life steps become **Gradus** (coin) and burned calories become **Ries** (energy). Strength from the warrior. (His gift rides on an older current: the Elder Road itself answers walking, section 2b. Valdric adds to a power that was always there.)
- **Seraphine the Kind**, mother of the slain Caedmon, guards Gradus's soul so he does not lose himself over the endless miles, and she caught Maelis's soul before it scattered and kept it whole in the Sunless Court. Endurance of spirit from the mourner.

### Vessels (LOCKED)

In Viatera, mortals who take up the road become **vessels**, carrying the spirit of Gradus or of Maelis while the two remain in the Sunless Court. The two gifts are temperaments, not sexes: **endurance** (the walker who cannot be stopped) and **clarity** (the seer who cannot be fooled). They map onto the existing Wanderer gender sprite as the common case (male Wanderer tends to Gradus/endurance, female to Maelis/clarity), but the binding is temperament, not gender, and known exceptions exist. Same legend, two halves.

### The player is not the only one (LOCKED, deepening C)

Vessels are not unique and the player is not a prophesied chosen one. Since the Anathema the dead have reached into many mortals. Most never awaken (they never notice the gift, or they break under it). Some who did awaken were hunted down by Mordric the Iron Hound. And some were **turned**, bent to Caelvorn's side, so a turned vessel is a tragic enemy the player may meet on the Road, someone who made the other choice. The player is the latest, not the last. They can fail and be replaced, which is exactly why the stakes are real.

### The awakening — how the player learns what they carry (LOCKED)

Since Caelvorn corrupted the Litany, the old clockwork blessings are gone and everyone must toil. For nearly all of them, toil drains: they walk and tire, work and break.

The player is the exception, and that is the whole reveal:

- **Gradus vessels** learn it through energized toil. When the player walks in the Realm of Toil and submits steps at the Keep (Realm Sync), the effort does not drain them; it returns as Ries, as power in Viatera. Toil makes them stronger, not weaker. That is not how a mortal works. That is how Gradus worked, the man whose curse turned every mile to iron. The energy is the proof. Staging: the player arrives in Oppidum among the weary, makes a first sync, and where everyone else slumps the player lights up. Someone who knows the old signs names it: *you carry Gradus.*
- **Maelis vessels** learn it through clarity. Maelis never walked Gradus's road; her gift is the eye that saw through a god wearing a borrowed face. The corrupted Litany still fools the faithful every dawn, but to a Maelis vessel its lies ring false. Their awakening is the morning they realize they are the only one in the square who can hear the king's poison under the holy words.

The two vessels are the two things Caelvorn fears most and the two things needed to resist him: the walker who cannot be stopped and the seer who cannot be fooled. Endurance and truth. Both share the same step/calorie loop and the same classes, so this stays in flavor (and a possible small passive), not a systems fork.

### Class patronage (LOCKED — full detail in section 14)

A vessel walks the road and, on committing to a calling at level 10, is answered by that calling's god, who lends aid: Knight to Valdric, Mage to Myrren, Archer to Aelric, Cleric to Seraphine. The patrons are the four Crowns, never Caelvorn (the High Crown is the enemy). Taking a calling is accepting an ally in the war in heaven, each helping for their own reason and at their own cost. See section 14.

## 7b. Maelis in the Sunless Court — the hidden architect (LOCKED)

Seraphine caught Maelis's soul and carried it into the Sunless Court, then hid her, because Caelvorn would destroy Maelis on sight to wound Gradus further. So husband and wife are now in the same realm and cannot meet. The Court is sunless, a vast dark, and Maelis is concealed even from Gradus. He does not know she was hidden on purpose; he thinks he simply has not walked far enough. So he walks, millions of miles through the dark, searching for a wife kept from him. (Orpheus inverted: both already in the underworld, unable to find each other.)

Both Gradus and Maelis are equally ascended and both actively fight Caelvorn; neither one's story serves the other. They are two fronts of the same war: the hammer and the eye.

**Maelis, the quiet war.** Using the eye that once saw through Caedmon's borrowed face, she perceives from within the Court what Caelvorn did to the Litany, understands the Anathema better than anyone, and reaches into the mortal world through the souls attuned to her (Maelis vessels), feeding them clarity, running the network, and working to undo the Anathema at its root. The forgotten woman, remembered by history only as the god-killer's dead wife, is the unseen architect of the rebellion. Her tragic choice: reveal herself to Gradus and risk Caelvorn ending both her and her work, or stay hidden and let the man she loves believe she is gone. She chooses the work, for now. The female-vessel path is Maelis's campaign, told from her side.

**Gradus, the loud war.** He is loose inside Caelvorn's own realm, the one thing in the king's house the king cannot control (shielded by Valdric's strength and Seraphine's guard over his soul). He wages open war in the Court: hunting and breaking what Caelvorn builds there, driving toward the High Crown's throne, all while searching the dark for Maelis. The power surges a Gradus vessel feels are Gradus fighting through them, deliberately.

## 7c. Gradus's flaw and the player's judgment (LOCKED)

Gradus believes that enough force and enough endurance overcome anything, because for him they always have. That very conviction killed a god and brought the Anathema down on the world, and he has not learned it. He wars the way he searches: walking it down, breaking through, never stopping to listen. It is exactly why he cannot find Maelis, who is hidden by stillness and concealment, the one thing brute persistence cannot reach. His greatest strength is the cause of his greatest losses. (Late payoff: some things are found only by standing still.)

**The player decides if he is a hero.** The Accounts are split on him already. A Gradus vessel especially must reckon: am I carrying the man who defied a tyrant god, or the reckless force whose rage doomed us all and who is still out there breaking things in the dark? The game never settles it; the player does, and town reactions and dialogue bend to where they land.

## 8. The four stances (LOCKED in shape, expandable)

All four Crowns voted that Gradus was a threat. Each hid a different reason, revealed over time.

- **Valdric the Iron** demanded the law be kept; a god was dead and strength must answer. In truth he had never admired a mortal more, and a creed built on earned strength cannot condemn the one man who perfected it. He became the strength in the road.
- **Myrren the Veiled** called him a danger to the order. Her real motive is study: a mortal who can kill a god is the one thing her knowledge never accounted for, and she wants to watch what he becomes more than she wants him gone. The long game, played cold.
- **Aelric Far-Sight** alone foresaw that a god walking the realm would unmake the gods, warned them, and was ignored. His "threat" vote was a push to let the consequence run its course, because he has seen a future where mortals must walk free of the Litany. Quietly subversive.
- **Seraphine the Kind** was expected to scream for vengeance for her murdered son. Instead she argued for the Sentence over erasure, kept Maelis's soul whole, and aids Gradus in secret. The most merciful of the four carries the heaviest grief.

## 9b. Caelvorn's wrath and the present day (LOCKED — this is the present-day engine)

Caedmon's father is **Caelvorn the Sovereign, the High Crown**, king above the Four Crowns, the god whose voice the Litany always was. For a thousand years, when mortals heard the Litany at dawn, they heard Caelvorn. He was the order itself.

When his son died on the road, Caelvorn did not grieve like his wife. He raged. He swore the **Anathema**: all of Viatera would pay for the blood of his son, and Gradus would pay most and longest, his name unmade and forgotten, his every step turned to suffering.

Then he made good on it the only way a king-god can. He turned his own voice against the people. The Litany that guided them for a thousand years stopped guiding and began to punish. Some mornings it falls silent. Some mornings it lies. The blessings that ran the world like clockwork curdled into curses. **This is the present-day wound, and it grows directly out of the murder.**

### Caelvorn's case (LOCKED, deepening A — he is not a cartoon)

He is wrong in what he does to the innocent, but he is not wrong that he was wronged. A mortal murdered his son. By the realm's oldest law no mortal may raise a hand to a god, and Gradus did, and now the world stamps the killer's name on its coins and sings him in its taverns. From where Caelvorn sits he is a grieving father and a king watching a death-cult grow around the man who killed his child, fighting to hold the order that fed and protected mortals for a thousand years. The player should never be able to fully dismiss him. The resistance is righteous and also, from one true angle, a cult built around a god-killer. Hold both.

### Why the coin is called Gradus (LOCKED — ties currency to the story)

For a thousand years mortals never chose anything; they obeyed the Litany and prospered. Caelvorn's wrath ended that. For the first time, the people of Viatera had to act for themselves, against a god, with no instructions and no king of their own. So they rallied behind a memory: Gradus, the one mortal who ever defied a god and was not broken, the man the High Crown most wanted erased.

They took the name Caelvorn swore to unmake and made it the most-spoken word in the realm. They struck their coin in his honor and called it **Gradus**. Every coin earned and spent is an act of defiance, a name the High Crown cannot silence because it is in every hand and on every tongue. He wanted Gradus forgotten; the mortals made him their money. This is why the player, walking in the Realm of Toil, mints Gradus with their steps: they keep alive the name a god condemned. (Ries, the breath/spent fire, is the energy of that same defiance.)

### The rift in the heavens (feeds section 8)

Seraphine the Kind, the mother, chose mercy against her own husband and secretly aids the man who killed her son, because she knows whose fault it truly was. The divine world now has a rift down its center: the High Crown's vengeance against the Queen's mercy, with the Four Crowns forced to choose sides. The four stances in section 8 were setting this up; now they have a war to take sides in.

### Caelvorn's agents on the ground (LOCKED — the faces the player fights)

Caelvorn is distant and vast, so he works through mortals and through the cracked seal between realms.

- **The broken door**: when Caedmon broke the covenant by walking the realm in flesh, the seal between realms cracked and never fully closed. This is how the Vagabond crosses, and how Caelvorn's agents cross too.
- **The Hollow Choir**: an order of priests who still read the corrupted Litany at dawn and enforce it. Some are deceived; some know exactly whose poison they speak. Around them gather quislings, mortals who heard the Anathema and chose to serve the wrathful king for safety or power rather than resist.
- **Mordric the Iron Hound** (LOCKED): Caelvorn's enforcer and hunter of vessels, who crossed through the broken door, the Vagabond's dark mirror. Late reveal: a son of Valdric the Iron, so the god of earned strength has two reflections in the world, Gradus (the mortal who earned it, revered) and Mordric (the god-born who serves a tyrant with it). He turns toward the player at the close of the opening chapter; he hunts across the Reaches thereafter. He has hunted vessels before this one, and turned some of them rather than killing them (section 7). His greatest trophy is **Roderic, the Second Strider** (section 9g), the vessel the Footsworn once believed was Gradus's equal, now first of the Hollowed.
- **Choirmaster Vane** (LOCKED): the Hollow Choir's leader in Oppidum, who reads the corrupted Litany each dawn and has convinced himself its cruelties are tests of faith. The player's first human antagonist. More frightening as a true believer than a knowing villain.

## 9d. Caedmon's death and the Bleeding Mile (LOCKED — the map's forbidden end)

When a man bleeds out, the ground forgets him. When a god bleeds out, the ground drinks him and goes wrong. Caedmon's blood soaked a stretch of the road and changed it. The beasts there did not flee the divine blood; they drank it, and it drove them frenzied, swollen, far stronger than they should be. Plants grow wrong; the air carries a god's rage that never cooled. The people call it **the Bleeding Mile**, formally **Caedmon's Wound**. Gated to level 40+, the far, forbidden end of the map.

Two things make it the climax of the geography:

- It sits past the Ruins, at the road's end, and that is no coincidence: this is the same place where Gradus, having killed Caedmon, kept walking until he crossed past the edge of the world into the Sunless Court. A god died here; a mortal vanished beyond the world here. Both miracles happened on the Mile.
- The heretic's Account ("the god is not dead") is half true here. Something of Caedmon lingers at the heart of the Mile, a remnant of divinity that never fully died, and its lingering rage is what drives the mobs mad. That remnant is the most wanted thing in Viatera: Caelvorn wants his son, Myrren the Veiled wants to study what a dying god becomes, heretics worship it. Untouchable until very late. A long-game raid/boss at the literal end of the road.

Continuity: two wounds run the present-day world. The **voice-wound** (the corrupted Litany, everywhere) and the **blood-wound** (the Bleeding Mile, localized but intense). Ruins first-clear relics can be fragments tied to Caedmon (crystallized godsblood, etc.). OPEN: whether the Mile is the deepest part of the Ruins zone or a separate zone gated behind it.

## 9c. Implementing unreliable history (design plan for section 10's note)

Store the myth as **Accounts**, not facts. Each lore entry carries a `source` and a `zone`; the same event has several conflicting entries shown side by side (Elden Ring style), never fully resolved. Example, the killing of Caedmon:

- **The Crownsong** (Caelvorn's faithful): Gradus the damned god-killer who broke the covenant and doomed us all.
- **The Greenwood telling**: Gradus the only man who ever stood up to a god.
- **A heretic's account**: there was no murder; the god is not dead.

Surface them three ways, all cheap to build as tagged text in a single-file PWA:

- Bards in the Inn sing zone-keyed ballads, so the hero is a martyr in one town and a monster two zones over.
- NPC dialogue contradicts itself between towns when the player asks about Gradus.
- Item and relic flavor text quietly disagrees with the songs.

The player collects Accounts and pieces together a truth the game never states outright. Later we can let player choices tilt which version a town believes about them.

Topics the codex should cover (each in several conflicting versions): the killing of Caedmon; the death of Maelis (was she innocent, did she love the god, did she even exist); Gradus's crossing into the Court (every version wrong, the mystery kept); why the coin bears his name; the true nature of the Litany (corrupted, or are mortals being justly punished); the Bleeding Mile (is the god dead or not); what vessels truly are (chosen, or superstition); who the Vagabond is; and Caelvorn's real motive.

Mechanic hook: certain entries carrying Maelis's truth are fully legible only to Maelis vessels (clarity is her gift). Gradus vessels see them smudged or self-contradictory.

## 9e. The opening chapter — "The Dawn That Lied" (LOCKED; prose drafted in Chapter-01-The-Dawn-That-Lied.md)

The root every story quest grows from. Built to teach the loop and launch the war in one sitting, and to work as a book's first chapter.

**The command:** the Litany names a sickness in Oppidum, blames the west well, and orders the town to cast everyone who drank from it out through the walls and into **the Hollow** (a cave beyond the town), then seal them in to spare the rest. Cruelty dressed as protection.

**The truth (the gut-punch):** the Hollow is where the broken door leaks. A thing crept through it, the **Tithe-Eater**, and the corrupted Litany has been feeding it for a season by sending it the town's sick. The dawn command was never protection; it was a sacrifice.

Beats:
1. The player wakes in Oppidum; the town gathers at the chapel for the dawn Litany, read by **Choirmaster Vane**. The voice gives the command above. The townsfolk obey, dull-eyed, because they have never once disobeyed. Open on that horror.
2. The player alone feels it is wrong. A Gradus vessel feels it in the body, a refusal rising in the legs; a Maelis vessel hears the lie plainly, the poison under the holy words.
3. At the crowd's edge, the **Vagabond** watches the player, not the temple, the only other person not entranced. He tells the player to do the thing no one has done since the god-killer: refuse.
4. To act, the player must walk. He sends them to the Wayward Keep to sync their first real steps at the Milestone, and the effort returns as power, the vessel-gift firing for the first time. The mechanical hook lands in the first ten minutes.
5. The player defies Vane, enters the Hollow (the chapter's cave), finds the exiled sick, and kills the **Tithe-Eater** (the chapter's boss), exposing the lie. Some townsfolk are grateful; others are terrified that someone defied the Litany. (Seed of unreliable history, reputation, and the local Footsworn.) The Vagabond names what the player is and vanishes. By the next dawn the Litany speaks the player's name, and far down the Road **Mordric the Iron Hound** turns to look. End the prologue here.

Tie-ins for build: cave = the Hollow; boss = the Tithe-Eater; first human antagonist = Choirmaster Vane; recurring hunter introduced = the Iron Hound.

## 9f. The Vagabond — the guide NPC (LOCKED)

A ragged traveler who walks like he has walked forever (a quiet clue that he mirrors Gradus). He knows the player's name before it is given, says too much and too little, and disappears and returns without explanation. For now, simply "the Vagabond."

Late reveal (LOCKED): he is a **son of Aelric Far-Sight**, the only Crown who foresaw the ruin and wanted mortals free of the Litany. A son of the seer walks among mortals through the broken door to guide vessels, subversive as his father. This sets him against the rival son who serves Caelvorn (section 9b).

His agenda is suspect (LOCKED, deepening C): Aelric wants mortals free of the gods entirely, a cause far larger than any one town or any single vessel, and the Vagabond serves that cause, not the player. His help is real, but his loyalty is to the war, not to the person carrying it. He may be willing to spend the player, or a town, for the longer game. The gentlest-seeming face in the story has the coldest long view.

## 9g. The Hollowed and the Second Strider (LOCKED — Ruins capstone)

**The Hollowed** (corrupted vessels): a mob family of former vessels who awakened and were then turned, by Mordric, by the corrupted Litany feeding on them, or by despair. They still carry the vessel-mark gone wrong and still move with the tireless vessel-gait, but hollow-eyed and hostile. Each one is a person who could have been the player. The hollowness motif runs through the game: the Hollow Choir (priests), the Hollow (cave/Tithe-Eater, Reach 1), the Hollowed (corrupted vessels, Reach 3).

**Roderic, the Second Strider** (LOCKED; name adjustable): the boss who holds the Ruins. Before the player awakened, he was the Footsworn's hope, a Gradus vessel who walked farther down the Road than anyone since Gradus himself, so far that many believed he was Gradus returned in the flesh, or his equal, the one who would reach the Mile and free the dead. Then he vanished, and the Accounts split on his fate (see ACCOUNTS.md). The truth waits in the Ruins: Mordric did not kill him, he broke and turned him, and Roderic is now the first and greatest of the Hollowed, the **Broken Strider** who holds the dead city.

Why he lands:
- He is the living end of Gradus's own flaw. He tried to force his way to the Court the way Gradus would, walked over everyone to do it, and shattered. Defeating him is the clearest warning a Gradus vessel can get: this is what you become if your patron's flaw goes unchecked.
- He is not mindless. He preaches a coherent, sympathetic despair: every vessel who fights only multiplies the world's suffering; Gradus's defiance is what froze the world; the merciful thing is to stop walking and let the gods have their order back. He offers the player that "mercy" before the fight. He is not lying about the suffering, only wrong about the answer. (Despair is the mechanism of the Hollowing itself: vessels are turned when hope runs out, not when strength does. See the Prologue and the "Common struggles" note in section 12.)
- He is Mordric's trophy and proof the Iron Hound can turn even the greatest. Beating him makes Mordric's hunt personal.

Placement: capstone of the Ruins (Reach 3, "truth surfaces"). He came to the dead city seeking what it knew (a way to the Court, a way to break the Anathema), forced it, and Mordric broke him there; the corruption spread from his fall, and the Hollowed now infest the Ruins. (Alternative placement: the Hoarmarch. Recommend Ruins.) Profile in CHARACTERS.md.

## 11. Prose & narrative structure (for the game-novel)

Goal: one artifact that reads as a novel and plays as a game, where progressing the game reads the novel in its intended order.

- **Spine — the Player-vessel thread.** Present-day Viatera, close third person (or "you" in-game, third in the novel). This is the main quest; everything the player does lives here.
- **Two interlude threads from the Sunless Court.** Gradus's chapters (the loud war + the search) and Maelis's chapters (the hidden war). In game they arrive as cutscenes / lore unlocks at milestones, so the player only learns what the dead are doing as they earn it.
- **The Accounts.** Songs, testimonies, and codex fragments about the deep past, chronicle voice, deliberately contradictory (Elden Ring item text + GoT histories). Ambient lore the player collects (see 9c).
- **Voice/tense.** Past-tense chronicle voice for the Accounts; close third for the living threads, so history and now feel different.

### Acts mapped to zones

Each Reach is a full multi-chapter arc (~5-7 chapters per 10 levels), not a single chapter. Chapter-by-chapter outlines live in **STORY-ARCS.md** (with a reusable per-Reach template). Summary:

- **Act I — Oppidum** (lv 1-10, six chapters): the awakening, "The Dawn That Lied," and the cost of the first defiance. Detailed in STORY-ARCS.md.
- **Act II — the Greenwood** (10-20): the resistance takes shape; first real enemies.
- **Act III — the Ruins** (20-30): the truth of the Anathema surfaces; the Hollowed and Roderic (9g).
- **Act IV — the Hoarmarch** (30-40): grief; Wendholt and the Frozen Ford, where the founding tragedy becomes landscape.
- **Endgame — the Bleeding Mile** (40-50): the dead-god remnant; the threshold to the Court.
- **Future expansion — the Sunless Court** (50-100, second book): walk with Gradus and Maelis; the war reaches Caelvorn; the long-dead families may appear.

## 12. World detail

### The Road and the Reaches (LOCKED — the spine of the map)

The realm is organized around one feature: an ancient pilgrim **Road** running the length of Viatera, from Oppidum at its head to the Bleeding Mile at its far end, the very road Gradus walked until he stepped off the edge of the world. To walk the Road is the act the whole game and faith are built on. The map is a road; the player is always moving along it.

The Road passes through five great stretches of country called **Reaches**, each its own land but bound by the Road and the old faith. The level-50 first release is the full length of the Road, five Reaches; the Sunless Court waits beyond for the expansions.

| Reach | Zone | Levels | Land | The arc |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Oppidum** | 1–10 | a clear blue lake ringed by green hills, distant mountains | the fraying present; the awakening |
| 2 | **The Greenwood** | 10–20 | lush forest, fast rivers, waterfalls, wildflower meadows | the wild gone feral without the Litany |
| 3 | **The Ruins** | 20–30 | a sun-bleached desert canyon holding a dead temple-city | truth surfaces; the Hollowed and Roderic (9g) |
| 4 | **The Hoarmarch** | 30–40 | an unnatural frozen land that should be green and temperate | personal grief: Wendholt and the Frozen Ford |
| 5 | **The Bleeding Mile** | 40–50 | a beautiful country gone wrong, blood-warped and haunted | the divine wound; the Road's end, finale of book one |

### The world is gorgeous (LOCKED — beauty is the rule, blight the exception)

Most of the world is stunning and the player should love being in it: bright rivers and deep vegetation, the desert's red grandeur, beaches and coasts and high mountains in the wider world beyond the Road. The horror works by contrast. The Anathema's cold and the blight are a localized wound eating paradise from the far end, not a grey filter over everything. The mood gradient is beauty giving way to wrongness as the player nears the Mile: the early and middle Reaches are lovely and only faintly touched by the creeping chill; the last two are where the blight has bitten deep. We walk through a dying paradise, which hurts more than walking through a ruin.

(The wider world holds biomes book one does not visit, coasts, beaches, great mountain ranges, reserved for later books.)

### Peoples and cultures (LOCKED — variety without new races)

The civilized world is human (see races decision below). Variety comes from distinct human cultures shaped by the gorgeous biomes: lake folk, forest folk, desert folk, mountain folk, each with their own ways, dress, speech, and quarrels. Martin's method, not the orc-elf-dwarf shelf. This is where the world feels broad and lived-in.

### Common struggles (LOCKED — relatable human pain inside the fantasy)

The post-Litany world is full of ordinary human struggle, and it is woven through every town, not decoration but stakes. People drink because freedom terrified them when the dawn voice broke. There is debt, and the Bank owns half of every town, and families like the Stranes grew rich while neighbours starved. There is a numbing herb folk chew against the cold and the fear. And there is despair, which is wired straight into the horror: **despair is what the Hollowing feeds on.** Vessels do not fall because they are weak; they fall because hope runs out, and the Iron Hound turns people by finishing a hope that was already dying (see the Prologue and 9g). So every grieving, broke, drinking soul in a tavern is a quiet stake in the war, and depression is not just texture, it is the spiritual disease at the center of the corruption.

### Peoples and races decision (LOCKED — humans only, for now)

No orcs, elves, dwarves, or the standard fantasy roster. Reasons: it is the most cliche move available and would drag a fresh premise onto the generic shelf; it dilutes the human heart the story runs on; it tangles the vessel/Litany lore for no gain. Variety comes from human cultures (above). Wonder and danger come from what we already have: the gods, the Hollowed, the Tithe-Eater, the blood-warped beasts of the Mile, things through the broken door. Any truly non-human thinking peoples are reserved for the Sunless Court and later books, and must be original to this world, never borrowed.

The Road is literally the path Gradus once walked (and walked off the end of), so the player retraces his journey Reach by Reach. Every zone can therefore hold a piece of his history, seeded when each is written.

### Governance — no kings (LOCKED — the alternative to kingdoms)

Viatera never needed kings, because the Litany was the only ruler it ever had. The gods governed directly through the dawn voice and every town obeyed. No crowns, no thrones, no royal lines, only the Road and the temples along it. That is why the corrupted Litany is so devastating: the people lost not a king but the only authority they ever knew, all at once, and most have no idea how to rule themselves.

Structure: the Road and its Reaches. Towns were historically overseen by a temple-appointed **Warden**. Leaderless now, each improvises: some fall to the Hollow Choir, some scrape together a council of elders, some look to whoever the Footsworn trust. Medieval feel through wardens, reeves, temples, and walled towns, plus a power vacuum that drives the whole conflict.

### Oppidum in detail (Reach 1)

Feel: a small walled waystation town at the head of the Road, on the shore of a wide grey lake. A few hundred souls, grey stone walls, timber-and-daub houses in muddy lanes, one cobbled square with the chapel steps at its heart, the Road running out the far gate. Prosperous once, when pilgrims flooded through to walk the Road; now half-shuttered and quiet. A town holding its breath, keeping the old routines hollowly, the way a body moves a moment after the spirit has gone.

Air and weather: still lovely. A clear blue lake, green hills, mountains soft on the horizon, warm days. The only wrong note is a faint chill at dawn that the oldest folk swear did not used to be there, the blight's first far whisper, easy to dismiss and impossible to forget once you've felt it. Oppidum is the gentlest, prettiest place the player will stand; the beauty thins and the wrongness grows the closer the Road runs to the Mile.

Smell and sound: baking bread (the one comfort that holds) over woodsmoke, tallow, wet stone, lake-weed, fish, and dung in the lanes, with a faint sourness under it that came with the corrupted Litany, like milk on the turn, that no one names though everyone has stopped drinking from certain wells. Sounds: the dawn bell, gulls and lapping water, the creak of the Road-gate, fewer voices than the square was built to hold.

Locations the player can enter:

- **The Chapel** — worn temple steps facing the square; the town gathers here at the dawn bell for the Litany. Site of "The Dawn That Lied." Sacred and faintly menacing, the one building still perfectly kept (the Hollow Choir tends it) while the rest of town frays.
- **The Wayward Keep (Realm Sync)** — an old watchtower at the Road's head where pilgrims once took their blessing. Inside stands the **Milestone**, an ancient standing stone that drinks the miles truly walked and pays them back as Gradus and Ries. The local point where the two realms touch; it hums when a vessel nears.
- **The Tavern: the Foundered Horse (quest board)** — the beloved one. Oppidum's loud, warm, funny heart, the cantina, the one room in town that is not afraid of anything. Its sign is a horse flat on its back with its legs in the air, looking delighted to be done, and nobody agrees why it is called that (a running argument that breaks into fights about twice a week). Bounties and pleas are pinned to a scarred oak slab by the door, but nobody comes for the board. They come for the life. Atmosphere: smoke, stew, spilled ale, music done badly and loudly, the light and noise of it a defiance of the cold dark outside. Thematically it is the anti-Hollowing: the place no one drinks alone.
  - **Big Doll** — the barkeep. A mountainous, perpetually unimpressed woman, once a strongwoman in a desert fair, who waters the ale and dares you to mention it, settles brawls by carrying both fighters out under her arms, and has heard every sad story in three Reaches twice. Soft heart, deeply buried.
  - **Wick and Gorn** — two ancient regulars at the corner table playing an endless game of stones, bickering, narrating and heckling everything that happens, never once finishing a game.
  - **Sweet Lyle** — the resident bard, who sings the Gradus ballads with the wrong words and the wrong tune and gets pelted with crusts, sparking the eternal tavern war over the "true" version (unreliable history as comedy).
  - **Pinch** — the Choir informer everyone knows is a Choir informer; the regulars feed him gloriously false gossip for sport and watch him scurry off to report it.
  - **Captain** — a three-legged dog who belongs to everyone and no one, steals food without shame, and is the most respected patron in the house.
- **The Inn (Maren's, rest / rooms)** — the quieter counterpart, lodging and a smaller common room, warmer and more intimate than the Foundered Horse, and lately touched by grief (Tam). Where the player rests and where the harder conversations happen.

> Taverns, the world over (LOCKED principle): every town has one, and a tavern is the hearth of the living, the recurring fun location readers and players love to return to, each with its own keeper, regulars, and running jokes. The Foundered Horse is the flagship and sets the tone. In a world being eaten by cold and despair, the tavern is where people are loudest, warmest, and least alone, which is exactly why it matters.

### Food & drink — the ember (LOCKED — the world's signature drink)

The **ember** is the realm's beloved drink, the warming alternative to ale: a hot, spiced, honeyed brew, frothed thick with a cap of toasted-honey foam, the colour of firelight through honey, with a fiery distilled spirit hidden under the sweetness. The froth is the kind part; what is under it will put you on the floor. It is the pilgrim's drink, because no one walks the cold Road on watered beer, and the spice that defines it comes up the trade routes from the desert Reach.

- Every tavern's ember is different and every keeper guards the recipe. **Big Doll's** ember at the Foundered Horse is famous and a little feared ("Doll's own has put bigger men than you on the floor and written to their mothers about it").
- A soft, unspiked version exists for children and the devout (a "soft ember" / "child's ember").
- Thematic charge: in a world eaten by cold and despair, an ember is a little fire you drink, the same small defiance the coin is. Recurs across the chapters as comfort, comedy, and a marker of the living.

### Food & cooking, the second pillar of warmth (LOCKED)

Food carries the same weight as the ember: in a freezing realm being eaten by despair, a hot shared meal is warmth and life against the cold and the dark, another **bulwark against the Hollowing** (a cooked meal restores hope/will the way the ember does; see section 16). Cooking is elevated from a life-skill to a thread of the living.

- **The everpot** (signature dish, stands beside the ember): a perpetual stew, never emptied, only added to, carried and re-cooked and shared, some of them generations old. Communal and enduring, the endurance theme made edible. Every hearth and tavern keeps one; the famous pots are ancient (the Foundered Horse's is decades old). A town's everpot going cold is a sign the town is dying.
- **Cuisine varies by culture/biome** (peoples and cultures, above): lake fish and eel, forest game and foraged greens, desert spice and flatbread. Food marks where you are and who you are among.
- **The hearth** (location): a roadside cookhouse where the road-weary are fed, a small bonfire that feeds you, kin to the tavern but humbler and scattered along the lonely Road. A recurring warm island.
- **Hob** (recurring cook, see CHARACTERS.md): a huge scarred ex-soldier who lost his taste for war and feeds people on the Road instead. Bridges the food pillar and the armies thread (a man who has seen armies and walked away from them).
- **The Inn (rest / rooms / rumors)** — beds, and a common room where a bard sings of an evening. The songs are the Accounts, and which Gradus he sings depends on who buys the ale.
- **The Bank (vault / investments)** — a squat stone vault and a careful keeper; the one place coin survives a bad death. Quiet tension: storing the Gradus coin is storing the name the High Crown swore to erase.
- **The Blacksmith** — melee arms, crossbows, the Forge, repairs; coal-smoke and quenching-steam, a trade thriving now that mortals must arm themselves.
- **The Woodsmith** — bows and staffs, quieter, resin-scented.
- **The Armorer** — plate and mail.
- **The Apothecary** — close and herb-thick, hung with drying bundles; potions brewed, gear enchanted.
- **The Commons** — the rough green and woodlot at the town edge: woodcutting, gathering, a little thieving.
- **The Lake** — Oppidum's cold grey shore, for fishing; deeper than it looks.
- **The Hunting Grounds** — open scrub beyond the walls; free hunts.
- **The Library** — a small dim room of salvaged books and pinned testimonies; home of the codex, where the player assembles the conflicting Accounts. A Maelis vessel reads here what a Gradus vessel sees only as smudges.

### Reach 4 — the Hoarmarch (LOCKED — the new fifth area)

A mournful borderland that should be green and temperate, locked instead in the unnatural winter the blight carried up the Road, the wrongness being that the cold has no business here at all. It holds the ruins of **Wendholt**, the village where Gradus and Maelis lived, and the **Frozen Ford** where Maelis drowned refusing a god. The player walks through the hero's dead home and the very ice his wife went under, the founding tragedy turned into landscape. Levels 30–40, the grief beat before the rage of the Bleeding Mile.

### Daily life after the Litany (the quiet tragedy)

For a thousand years the Litany told people when to sow, wed, and walk, and they prospered without ever choosing. Now the voice lies or goes silent, and mortals must farm, hunt, build, and decide for the first time in their history. Most are terrified of the freedom. This is the soil the resistance grows in.

### Factions

- **The Footsworn** (LOCKED) — the resistance who rally behind Gradus's name, mint and carry his coin as defiance, and walk the Road as faith and rebellion at once.
- **The Hollow Choir** — Caelvorn's priests who still read the poisoned Litany at dawn (section 9b).
- **The Wakeful** (LOCKED) — "those the lie cannot reach," Maelis's hidden cell, the few who carry her clarity, see the lie, and work quietly against it. Never named openly; the only ones who suspect the dead are still fighting.

## 10. Editor's review (2026-06-13) — grade: B-, strong foundation, one weakness and a missing half

What works: the central metaphor is rare and real — walking as devotion, the punishment that builds instead of breaks, effort itself as the sacred act — and it fuses to the fitness loop so the lore and gameplay justify each other. Caedmon as careless rather than evil is the right instinct. The four stances give moral grey. The vessel mechanic earning its place in the myth is clever.

The weakness — Maelis was fridged (a woman killed only to fuel a man's rage, with no arc of her own). The merged death now gives her agency, but it is not fully fixed until she has her own thread. The seam is already there: female vessels carry her, and her soul sits whole in the Sunless Court. The female-vessel path should be HER arc, not an echo of his. Open question: what is Maelis doing in the Court that Gradus does not know about? She should not be waiting to be rescued.

The missing half — RESOLVED 2026-06-13. The present-day engine is now Caelvorn's Anathema (section 9b): the High Crown's vengeance corrupted the Litany, the world is unraveling, and the living antagonist is the king of the gods himself. The threat grows directly out of the murder, and it gives the player a reason to walk now. Still to do: define how the corrupted Litany shows up moment to moment in play, and who serves Caelvorn on the ground (his agents / faithful) as the antagonists the player actually meets.

What a bestseller still needs here:
- A protagonist with a flaw, not a blameless everyman. Candidate: Gradus's refusal to stop is also a refusal to grieve; the endless walking is avoidance as much as devotion.
- A living antagonist with sympathetic logic (Caedmon is gone; someone must oppose the player now and believe they are right).
- Unreliable history (GoT / Elden Ring): towns disagree on whether Gradus is a hero or the god-killer who damned them by breaking the covenant. Different songs, different truths.
- A theme bigger than revenge: endurance, grief, whether effort is ever rewarded, free will against the Litany. Revenge is the surface, not the engine.

## 14. The Four Callings and their patrons (LOCKED in shape; ability numbers are build-phase tuning)

Two layers of identity. The **dead spirit** carried (Gradus or Maelis, endurance or clarity) is fixed at the start (section 7). The **living patron** is chosen at level 10 when a calling claims the vessel.

The six callings form a spectrum of how a soul stands toward the gods. Four are **allied to a Crown** (Knight, Mage, Archer, Cleric). At one extreme stands the **Paladin**, bound to a god by total oath. At the other stands the **Unsworn** (the dagger calling), bound to no god at all. Patrons are the four Crowns, never Caelvorn. Each aids for their own reason, with its own color and cost (ties to section 8 stances and CHARACTERS.md).

### Knight — sworn to Valdric the Iron
- **Why he helps / the catch:** Valdric loves strength fairly earned and is already the secret strength in Gradus's road, so he aids most honestly, fewest strings. But the Knight walks Gradus's own path, force and endurance, the path that hollowed Roderic (9g). Strongest calling, most at risk of the long fall. The Ruins' warning made personal.
- **Affinity:** sword and shield, heavy melee.
- **Passive — Ironward:** the longer a fight runs, the harder the Knight is to put down; strength rises with the burden, as the weight fed Gradus.
- **Active — the Unbroken Vow:** plant and refuse to fall, turning a killing blow and answering it.

### Mage — sworn to Myrren the Veiled
- **Why she helps / the catch:** Myrren serves only her hunger to understand; a vessel who carries a god-killer and wields the deep words is the most interesting thing in an age, so she aids to watch. Her gifts carry hidden strings, and she may feed the enemy a secret as readily as the player. Most powerful knowledge, most dangerous patron.
- **Affinity:** staff.
- **Passive — the Veil:** perceives what is hidden (an enemy weakness, a lie's seam).
- **Active — a Deep Word:** a fragment of the unmaking-speech that breaks what it names; devastating, never quite free of cost.

### Archer — sworn to Aelric Far-Sight
- **Why he helps / the catch:** Aelric wants mortals free of the gods entirely; an archer's distance and foresight serve that cause, and his son the Vagabond already walks beside the player. His aid points toward freedom, but his war is larger than any one life and he will spend the small to win the large.
- **Affinity:** bow.
- **Passive — Far-Sight:** sees the blow before it lands; foresight as evasion and killing angle.
- **Active — the Foreseen Shot:** an arrow loosed at where the enemy will be, that does not miss.

### Cleric — sworn to Seraphine the Kind
- **Why she helps / the catch:** Seraphine keeps Maelis's soul whole and guards Gradus; of all patrons she gives most freely and asks least. The catch is sorrow, not a string: her mercy is drawn from her own strength, spent against her own husband. The kindest god in the cruelest position.
- **Affinity:** blunt arms, the mace.
- **Passive — the Vigil:** does not fall the first time they should (kept up once per fight), as she keeps the dying a moment longer.
- **Active — Mercy:** a deep restoration for the living; turned on the Hollowed, a release, since to a corrupted vessel an ending is the only kindness left.

### Paladin — the calling of the holy oath
- **The twist:** the only god who ever commanded holy warriors was Caelvorn, through the Litany, so historically a paladin was the High Crown's armored fist. When the Litany turned, most paladins turned with it. The Hollow Choir's enforcers are fallen paladins, and Mordric the Iron Hound is the greatest of them. A **player Paladin** is a renegade who took the same total oath but tore it from the High Crown and swore it instead to a rebel Crown (Valdric or Seraphine), or to the rising new faith around Gradus's name.
- **The cost:** the oath gives more than a Knight's alliance, but it binds. A broken paladin-oath is a catastrophic fall (a fallen player-paladin could become a Hollowed-like enemy). The class lives on one question: can faith be holy when the god it was built for became a monster.
- **Affinity:** blessed longsword or warhammer, heavy armor.

### Unsworn — the dagger calling
- **The stance:** the only calling that bends to no god at all. Their power comes not from heaven but from the **Elder Road** (the older, godless thing beneath the world, section 2b) and from clarity, the eye for the unseen weak point. The blade of the Wakeful and the free, distrusted by the faithful of every side, answering to no dawn. In a world where every other soul served a voice, the Unsworn serve only the truth and the edge.
- **Affinity:** daggers, stealth. (Player-facing label OPEN: Shade / Rogue / Stray.)

### The choosing (level 10)
A scene, not a menu. The vessel has walked far enough to be noticed in heaven, and the calling answers in its own register: Valdric as a weight settling and iron in the blood; Myrren as a whisper and a veil drawn back; Aelric as the horizon snapping into focus; Seraphine as warmth and a hand on the shoulder. For the **Paladin** it is the binding of an oath, heavier and more final than the rest. For the **Unsworn** no god answers at all; instead the Road itself answers, the old hum of the Milestone rising in the blood, and the silence where a god should be is the whole point.

### Vessel × calling (twelve readings)
The two layers (dead spirit × calling) combine into character. Examples: a **Gradus-vessel Knight** is the purest force build, strongest and walking Roderic's exact road (constant dread). A **Maelis-vessel Cleric** is clarity and mercy together, the gentlest path, sees the Hollowed for what they are. A **Maelis-vessel Unsworn** pairs the eye that cannot be fooled with a blade beholden to no god, the truest free agent in the world. A **Gradus-vessel Paladin** binds unstoppable endurance to a total holy oath, the most zealous and the most dangerous if the oath ever breaks. All twelve should read as different people.

## 15. Signature relics & weapons (LOCKED — every legend has a known object)

Principle: major figures, especially gods and legendary mortals, each carry a known signature item. Many become relics or unique items in the game; the "true" ones are often lost, faked, or fought over (good quest fuel).

- **Gradus's Sword** — his grandfather's plain rusted blade off the wall over the door, no warrior's weapon, that hacked down the god Caedmon. Ordinary iron made the holiest and most-counterfeited relic in the world: prayed over, fought over, faked by every blacksmith with a debt. The true blade's whereabouts are unknown (a long quest). Folk also half-worship his worn boots and the Road itself, but the Sword is the thing.
- **Maelis's Ring** — the plain band lost beneath the ice of the Frozen Ford when she drowned. Said to surface only for those with the clear sight. Prized by the Wakeful, and recoverable in the Hoarmarch (Reach 4).
- **Valdric the Iron — the First Hammer**, with which he beat out the first sword; a smith-god's hammer, the model of every Knight's faith in earned strength.
- **Myrren the Veiled — the Veil** (none have seen her face) and **the Deep Book** of unwritten words, the source a Mage's Deep Word is torn from.
- **Aelric Far-Sight — the Farbow** and the arrow that goes where the loosing eye has already seen it land.
- **Seraphine the Kind — the Vigil**, the lantern she keeps lit by the dying; the Cleric's passive is named for it.
- **Caelvorn the High Crown — the High Crown** (fused to the bone) and **the Litany** itself, his voice turned weapon.
- **Caedmon — the Borrowed Face**, the glamour he wore to deceive Maelis at the altar; and the lingering remnant of him at the Bleeding Mile.
- **Roderic the Second Strider — the Second's Banner**, the standard the Footsworn once marched under singing his name, now a thing they will not raise; and his great vessel's blade, carried still by the Hollowed thing he became.
- **Mordric the Iron Hound — the chains** he uses to leash the turned, and a heavy headsman's blade. The chains are the motif: he does not always kill, he keeps.
- **The Vagabond — his boots**, worn impossibly thin, the same way Gradus's were, which is a clue to anyone watching closely.
- **Orris the whisperer — his letters**, the web of correspondence through which he knows everything (section: Orris in CHARACTERS.md).

## 16. Tone, difficulty, and the Hollowing spiral (FromSoft register — LOCKED)

The story and the world are pitched for a hard, melancholy, Souls-grade experience, not a casual one. The mix of dark and funny is deliberate and they sharpen each other.

- **Hostile, indifferent, lonely world by default.** Alone, cold, and in danger is the baseline; most of the Road is solitary and brutal. Warmth and company (taverns, hearths, the ember, a companion) are rare, fragile, hard-won islands, the "bonfires," and matter precisely because the dark is so total. This **reframes the connection theme** (don't walk alone) as precious-because-rare, not cozy.
- **Lethal stakes, missable tragedy.** No one is safe; the beloved die; NPC arcs can end badly or be missed entirely.
- **Cryptic lore in the game.** Item descriptions, environment, half-mad NPCs, the contradictory Accounts; players piece it together and theorize. The novel may be more explicit; the game withholds hard.
- **Bittersweet, ambiguous endgame**; heightened moral ambiguity (Caelvorn's case, the Vagabond's agenda).

### The Hollowing spiral (ties the theme to the hard-game loop)

- **What advances hollowing:** death, cold, and isolation. The corruption feeds on despair (established Ch4/Ch6); a soul left alone in the dark long enough is whispered down and emptied (becomes one of the Hollowed, 9g).
- **What holds it back:** warmth, will, and connection. The **ember** (section 12) is the bulwark, the little fire you drink to stay yourself, the world's equivalent of Souls "humanity." Rare warm places (the Foundered Horse and its kin) are the bonfires that restore you.
- **Vessel death is not final:** the dead keep pulling a vessel back (because the dead reached into them in the first place), but every return costs a little hope, and hope is exactly what the Hound and the cold hunt. Die enough, alone, in the cold, and you hollow. This is the hard-game loop, fully lore-justified, with the central theme load-bearing on the mechanics.

## 17. Armies and the scale of war (LOCKED — escalation, not constant)

The conflict is intimate by default (one vessel, one town, one hunter), which suits the Souls-feel Reach. Armies arrive by escalation, as climactic punctuation in Book One and as Book Two's canvas, never as the everyday. Keep the moment-to-moment Road lonely and hard so the big battles hit like weather breaking.

The factions become proto-armies and grow:

- **The Footsworn** turn from a frightened cell into a force. In the Greenwood (Reach 2) they "truly form" and gain a **war-leader** (new character, see CHARACTERS.md). By the Bleeding Mile they are something that can stand in a line.
- **The Hollow Choir's armed arm** (its fallen paladins) appears as a military threat under a **captain** (new character), the Choir's iron fist, distinct from Vane's soft menace.
- **The Hollowed** become a host. By the Ruins (Reach 3), Roderic commands them; the Hound's turned vessels are an army that wears familiar faces.

Scale by stage:
- Reaches 2-4: skirmishes, bands, ambushes, a growing dread of larger forces gathering.
- **Reach 5 (Bleeding Mile, Book One climax):** the first true epic battle, the Footsworn and allies against the Choir's host and the Hound's Hollowed, at the threshold of the Court.
- **Book Two (the Sunless Court):** full epic war, armies of the dead, the gods' hosts, Caelvorn against the rebel Crowns, Gradus's loud war made literal. Generals of the dead and the gods are introduced here.
