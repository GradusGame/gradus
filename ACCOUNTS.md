# The Accounts — codex / in-world lore (first batch)

These are the conflicting testimonies the player collects in Viatera (Library, Inn songs, NPC talk, item text). No entry is "true." Each carries a `source` and is meant to be read against the others. Entries tagged **[Wakeful]** carry Maelis's clarity and are fully legible only to a Maelis vessel; a Gradus vessel sees them smudged or self-contradicting.

Voice: chronicle / song / testimony, past tense. The player assembles the truth the game never states.

---

## ACCOUNT I — The Killing of the God

**Source: the Crownsong, sung by the Hollow Choir at dawn.**
There was a man who would not bow. When the blessed son walked among us in mercy, the man raised his hand against him and spilled what no mortal hand should touch. For that breaking the warmth went out of the world, and the cold that eats the Road is his doing, and his name is a wound we are made to carry. Speak it and you share his sin.

**Source: a Greenwood ballad, the long version, third verse.**
He chased a god on foot and the god rode, and still the god could not outrun him. Not for hate. For grief. He was no oathbreaker. He was the only man in a thousand years who looked at a god and said no, and the gods have hated us for it ever since, because now we know that it can be said.

**Source: a leaf nailed to the Ruins gate, hand unknown.**
You have all been told there was a death. There was no death. A god cannot die. He is in the Mile, and he is awake, and he is waiting for the ones the Choir sends him.

## ACCOUNT II — The Woman of Wendholt

**Source: common telling, Oppidum market.**
She was a farmwife out by the cold fords, the prettiest in the Reach they say, and a god took a liking to her, and she drowned. That is the whole of it. The rest is for the singers.

**Source: the Crownsong, the verse the Choir prefers.**
She turned a god's eye with her beauty and her pride, and what came of it came justly. Do not weep for the woman of Wendholt. Weep for the order she helped to break.

**[Wakeful] Source: a testimony copied in a hand that fades when a Gradus vessel reads it.**
She was not taken. She went. She crossed the ice on her own feet to stand at the altar and refuse him to his face, and the god who wore a borrowed mouth could not make her kneel. The ford he froze is what took her, not his hand. She died unbroken, and a thing that dies unbroken does not always stay where the dead are put. She sees still. Look for her in the places the lie will not cover.

**Source: the Testament of Wyn, her sister, set down in the old years and copied many times since (the words drift with the copying).**
They will tell you she was perfect. She was not. She was certain, and her certainty walked her onto the ice, and I told her so and she went anyway. I envied her every day of our lives and I would give the rest of mine to have her back. I am the last who knew her face before the singers got hold of it. Believe me before you believe a song: she was kinder than she was beautiful, and she was very beautiful, and she should not have gone alone.

## ACCOUNT III — Why the Coin Bears His Name

**Source: a Footsworn, asked plainly at the Tavern.**
Because the High Crown swore to unmake his name, and we are stubborn. Every coin in your hand is his name. Spend it, earn it, lose it, the name only spreads. You cannot burn a word that is in every pocket in the Reaches. That is the whole trick of it, and it is a good trick.

**Source: Choirmaster Vane, in sermon.**
You stamp your filthy little coins with a damned man and you wonder why the season will not turn. Stop minting the wound. Bring your coin to the Chapel and let it be melted clean, and perhaps the warmth comes back.

**Source: a Library marginal note, scholar unknown.**
Origin disputed. Some hold the first coins were struck in Oppidum in the cold's first winter; some in the Ruins long before. What is agreed is that no authority decreed it. The people named their money themselves, which has never once happened under the Litany, and may matter more than the name they chose.

## ACCOUNT IV — The Litany

**Source: the Hollow Choir, the catechism.**
The voice has spoken since before the first stone of the first town. It told us when to sow and when to wed and when to walk, and we prospered. It speaks still. That you find its words hard now is not the voice's fault. It is yours. Obey, and be kept.

**[Wakeful] Source: scratched inside a chapel bench, legible to the clear-eyed.**
Stand in the square at dawn and listen past the words. There is another sound under them now, like a man speaking through a wall, and what he wants is not what the words say. The voice did not break. It was turned. Whoever can hear the turning is not mad. They are the only one awake.

**Source: an old woman of Oppidum, who will not give her name.**
My grandmother said the Litany was always a hard master, and we only loved it because it fed us. Now it does not feed us, so we hear it true. Maybe nothing changed at all. Maybe we only opened our ears.

## ACCOUNT V — The Vanishing

**Source: three tellings, set side by side by a Library hand who could not choose.**
The first says he walked into the lake at Oppidum and did not come out. The second says the Road simply ended under his feet and he kept walking on nothing until the dark took him. The third says the gods reached down and dragged him up to answer for the god he killed. They cannot all be right. The keeper of this shelf suspects they are all wrong, and that the truth of where Gradus went is the one thing in Viatera no living mouth has ever held.

---

## ACCOUNT VI — The Cold

**Source: a Footsworn outrider.**
Walk the Road far enough and the frost climbs into your chest and will not leave. It is worse every league south, and it is a little worse in Oppidum every winter than the last. The cold is coming up the Road to us. Whatever sits at the end of it is breathing this way.

**Source: the Crownsong.**
The cold is the god-killer's debt, and we pay it. Give up his name and his coin and the season will turn. The Choir promises it.

**[Wakeful] Source: a single line, in the fading hand.**
The cold began where a god bled, not where a man sinned. Follow the cold to its coldest place and you will find what they do not want found.

## ACCOUNT VII — The Second Strider

**Source: an old Footsworn marching song, the verses they used to sing.**
There came a man who walked so far the Road forgot to end, and we called him the Second, for he strode as the First once strode, and we swore that he would reach the Mile and bring our dead ones home. Sing for the Second Strider. Sing him all the way down.

**Source: the same song, the way it is sung now, if it is sung at all.**
Do not sing the Second. Lay the verse down. He walked into the dead city and a colder thing walked out, and it wears his face, and it knows our names because once it marched beside us. Sing the First if you must sing. Never the Second.

**Source: the Crownsong, pleased.**
Behold your best. You crowned a man the equal of the god-killer and the Road hollowed him out like all the rest. This is the only end the murderer's path has ever had. Bring your coin to the Chapel and be spared it.

**[Wakeful] Source: scratched on a fallen stone in the Ruins.**
He was not killed. He was broken and turned, and what he says to you in the dark of the dead city will be partly true, which is the trap of him. The suffering he speaks of is real. The surrender he offers is not mercy. Do not take the rest he holds out. It is the rest that does not end.

**Source: a survivor of the Ruins, who will not go back.**
He did not strike at me first. He talked. He told me to set it down, all of it, the walking and the fighting and the hoping, and for the length of one breath I wanted to, more than I have ever wanted anything. That is what you should be afraid of. Not his hands. His kindness.
